# Calup application design review — 2026-10-19

**Design health: 22/40 — Acceptable; significant improvements needed.** This is a provisional source-based assessment. Browser inspection was attempted but could not complete. It is not a visual sign-off, usability study, performance benchmark, or WCAG compliance certificate.

The user confirmed review of merchant, customer, admin, and marketing surfaces; a clear, dependable, efficient personality; preservation of lime/black identity; avoidance of decorative clutter; and WCAG AA as the assessment target. [Strategic context](../../PRODUCT.md). Application source was not changed.

## Overall impression and anti-pattern verdict

Calup has recognizable identity and unusually careful financial safeguards, but the interface is less consistent than its underlying workflows. The biggest opportunity is to make promises, status, and interactive affordances reliable throughout the journey. Preserve the brand and simplify its application.

The qualitative assessment found moderate template symptoms: repeated rounded cards, nested panels, border-plus-wide-shadow combinations, floating hero success cards, repeated feature grids, section eyebrows, and route/card entrance animations. These are reasons to refine composition rather than discard the identity. Different marketing and operational densities are appropriate; inconsistent controls and inaccessible modal behavior are not.

The deterministic scan returned **8 findings**: 3 layout transitions, 3 font warnings, 1 side-stripe accent, and 1 bounce token. The font warnings are not defects by themselves: the product register permits Inter. The bounce token is unused. The onboarding transition references an undefined duration token, so it does not establish visible animation. The scan therefore supports targeted token/component cleanup, not a claim that eight visible defects were observed. [Raw evidence and interpretation](design-review-evidence/README.md).

## Priority issues

### 1. P1 — Reconcile promises with the actual offer

**Evidence:** [landingContent.js](../../frontend/src/data/landingContent.js), lines 35 and 169, promises free early access; lines 76 and 115 describe manual screenshot payments and online card payments as forthcoming. [SubscriptionHero.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/SubscriptionHero.jsx), lines 21 and 45, describes 30-day access with price after sign-in. [Onboarding.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/Onboarding.jsx), around line 935, requires activation before publishing. [BookingPage.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/booking/BookingPage.jsx), around line 554, implements conditional Cashfree checkout. Deployment settings determine which payment path is active; the public claims still need alignment.

Booking time-slot dots are generated from row index (`4 + index % 3`, around BookingPage line 1007), rather than capacity data. Their apparent availability/scarcity meaning is unsupported.

**Impact:** First-time merchants cannot confidently predict cost or requirements; customers can interpret decorative dots as real availability.

**Recommendation:** Use one explicit account of price, early-access eligibility, activation requirements, and supported payment methods. Publish price before account creation when authoritative pricing is available; otherwise explain why it is unavailable. Remove capacity dots or bind them to real data with an explained meaning.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable clarify` on public offer, onboarding activation, and booking availability.

### 2. P1 — Standardize accessibility across core tasks

**Evidence:** Div overlays in [Services.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/dashboard/Services.jsx), line 522; [Appointments.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/dashboard/Appointments.jsx), line 2001 onward; and [CustomerBooking.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/booking/CustomerBooking.jsx), line 1969 onward, lack the established native dialog pattern. The Appointments Escape listener closes dropdowns, not these modals. Booking form labels around lines 1108–1148 lack input associations/error descriptions; availability start/end inputs also need day-specific names. Analytics canvases at [Analytics.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/dashboard/Analytics.jsx), lines 178 and 199, lack text/table alternatives.

Source color calculations identify concrete readability weaknesses:

| Element | Source pair | Contrast |
|---|---|---:|
| Auth placeholder, Auth.css:79 | #979e90 on white | 2.76:1 |
| Auth switch text | #7a8273 on white | 3.99:1 |
| Policy helper text, Policies.jsx:165 | #888 on white | 3.54:1 |
| WhatsApp share label, BookingPageMgmt.jsx | White on #25D366 | approximately 1.98:1 |
| Global focus outline, global.css:112 | #C6F135 on white | 1.31:1 |

These are calculated declared pairs, not sampled rendered pixels. Normal text needs 4.5:1; relevant focus/non-text indicators need adequate adjacent contrast, generally 3:1. Auth-specific darker focus overrides exist, so the global focus concern should not be applied indiscriminately.

**Impact:** Keyboard, screen-reader, and low-vision users face avoidable friction in booking, scheduling, editing, and understanding data.

**Recommendation:** Reuse native dialogs with names, appropriate initial focus, Escape handling, and focus return. Associate labels/errors; expose full dates and selected state; provide chart data tables. Correct contrast with semantic tokens and verify in-browser. Keep labels visible; auth login/signup/recovery already have associated labels.

**Suggested commands:** `$impeccable audit` and `$impeccable harden` on shared forms, dialogs, calendar, and analytics.

### 3. P1 — Make status and success feedback authoritative

**Evidence:** [CashfreePaymentStatus.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/booking/CashfreePaymentStatus.jsx), lines 71–77, derives pending by excluding final states, including when attempt is null. It can then say “Confirming your payment” and “Your slot remains protected.” Its one-second timer updates local countdown time; it does not fetch payment state. The cancel-and-restart handler around line 121 checks state but does not navigate to restart. These are source-derived behaviors, not observed payment sessions.

[Availability.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/dashboard/Availability.jsx), lines 94–113, catches a failed provider availability save, logs it, and then displays “Availability updated.” [BookingPageMgmt.jsx](../../frontend/src/pages/dashboard/BookingPageMgmt.jsx), lines 22–24, reports a copied link without awaiting successful clipboard completion.

**Impact:** Users may wait under false reassurance, believe unsaved hours are published, or share a link that was never copied.

**Recommendation:** Model loading, unknown/unavailable, missing session, verification pending, expired hold, paid, and review-required explicitly. Promise slot protection only from authoritative state, show last check and next action, and use bounded reconciliation or explicitly manual checking. Make restart behavior match its label. Show success only after save/copy succeeds; preserve edits and offer retry/manual copy on failure.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden` on payment recovery and merchant save/share feedback.

### 4. P2 — Resolve mobile sizing risks before visual polish

**Evidence:** Analytics line 160 uses `minmax(360px, 1fr)`; Policies line 142 uses a 340px minimum. The padded mobile merchant workspace hides horizontal overflow in [DashboardLayout.css](../../frontend/src/layouts/DashboardLayout.css), lines 341 and 355. At a 320px viewport those minima exceed available content width; actual clipping was not rendered. CalendarWidget navigation controls around lines 69 and 79 are 24px square.

**Recommendation:** Make tracks shrink to available width, for example `minmax(min(100%, 360px), 1fr)`, and adapt financial forms/tables deliberately. Target 44px practical touch areas; distinguish that usability target from WCAG AA's applicable 24px minimum and exceptions. Verify 320/390px, tablet, desktop, 200% zoom, long service names, and device keyboard visibility.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable adapt` on merchant Policies, Analytics, scheduling, and customer calendar.

### 5. P2 — Simplify daily operations and align affordances with behavior

**Evidence:** The active topbar in [DashboardLayout.jsx](../../frontend/src/layouts/DashboardLayout.jsx) presents “Search for anything…” without functional search state/submission; its filter has no handler, and the Notifications bell navigates to Appointments. The settings avatar uses a click-only div. Desktop navigation has accessible names/tooltips but relies on icons visually. Appointments presents seven status tabs plus another status dropdown and service/date/sort controls.

**Impact:** Decorative controls cost attention without helping; overlapping filters make the current view harder to explain.

**Recommendation:** Remove unavailable controls or implement and label their exact scope. Use a real button/link for the avatar. Consolidate status filtering, expose active filters and a clear reset, prioritize today's work, and group secondary history/financial actions. Existing comments say navigation enhancements were previously disabled by user request: do not automatically restore them as a fix.

**Suggested commands:** `$impeccable distill` and `$impeccable clarify` on merchant shell and appointments.

## Cognitive load, emotional journey, and personas

The first visit offers useful booking outcomes but competes with repeated cards/mockups and inconsistent offer details. Setup asks for sustained commitment across eight stages; save/exit helps, but users should understand what remains before publication. Daily appointments have more than four ungrouped status choices plus overlapping filters. A calendar naturally has many choices; its problem is insufficient naming and unexplained decorative indicators rather than the date count itself.

The sharpest emotional valley is payment uncertainty. At that moment, the interface must answer: did money move, is the slot reserved, who owns the exception, and when should the customer check again? Confirmed receipts, booking IDs, print/save, and management links already provide a useful endpoint.

- **Sam, keyboard/screen-reader user:** Native support dialogs work differently from service/appointment overlays; date labels and chart alternatives are weak.
- **Casey, interrupted mobile customer:** Small calendar controls and payment countdown without background status reconciliation make recovery harder.
- **Jordan, first-time merchant:** Free marketing followed by billing/activation requirements, icon navigation, and inert search weaken confidence.

## Smaller findings and false-positive controls

- Reduce excessive rounding, nested boxes, decorative shadows, and repeated entrance motion after functional issues. Do not treat every card or familiar font as a defect.
- Landing reveal CSS defaults content to opacity zero until observer enhancement. Default content visible; use motion as progressive enhancement.
- A global reduced-motion CSS override exists in Landing.css around line 2247 and is eagerly imported. File-local absence does not prove CSS motion is unguarded. StatCard's JavaScript count-up timer has no corresponding preference guard and warrants separate treatment.
- Undefined success/warning 700 color tokens and `--transition-smooth` create styling debt; runtime visibility must be checked before assigning higher severity.
- ThemeProvider forces light mode; unused dark styles are not evidence of an exposed dark-mode defect.
- Light theme token aliases resolve the page background to white, not a cycle. Document actual resolved roles rather than relying on token names.
- Auth labels are associated; do not generalize booking-form label defects to every form.
- Source declarations of width/height transitions are not measured jank. No performance claim was established.
- No P0 blocker was established by this review. Absence of a confirmed P0 does not establish that every runtime flow works.

## Recommended sequence and acceptance evidence

1. Resolve P1 offer/status truth and accessibility issues. Acceptance: one pricing/payment account, no fabricated capacity, accurate missing/expired payment states, failed saves never say success, keyboard-completable named forms/dialogs, chart alternatives and passing contrast.
2. Address mobile reflow and task hierarchy. Acceptance: 320/390px and zoom checks, keyboard-open forms, long content, no clipped financial actions, one clear appointment filter model, truthful shell controls.
3. Consolidate visual tokens/components and polish. Preserve lime/black, tighten operational typography, reduce decorative repetition, and retain useful native controls. `$impeccable document` can capture the resulting visual system in DESIGN.md; none was generated in this review.

Open decisions: should exact price be public before signup, and which appointment task should dominate the merchant workspace? These shape a later implementation brief; this report does not authorize or implement changes.
